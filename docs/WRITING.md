# WRITING — MindWP voice and public language

This document guides public copy. It protects clarity, credibility, privacy, and tone without forcing every page into the same structure.

Identity belongs in [FOUNDATION.md](./FOUNDATION.md), the offer in [STRATEGY.md](./STRATEGY.md), and page roles in [PAGES.md](./PAGES.md).

## The voice

MindWP should sound:

- clear and observant
- calm and commercially serious
- confident without exaggerated claims
- experienced without self-importance
- warm, concise, and direct
- more like a senior builder than a marketing copy generator

Use “we” and “you.” Prefer concrete language, natural contractions, and varied sentence length. Do not polish every paragraph into identical three-part marketing phrases.

## The writing priority

Help the visitor understand three things:

1. MindWP builds visually accomplished websites.
2. Those websites can connect to practical enquiry handling.
3. The exact website, setup, handover, or management scope is shaped around the client.

Everything else is supporting detail.

## Copy and visual design share the work

Copy must not carry the whole experience. The page can use composition, interface demonstrations, project imagery, motion, diagrams, transitions, and interaction to create understanding and desire.

- Keep copy shorter when the visual already explains the point.
- Let project work appear large enough to be judged.
- Do not surround a strong visual with paragraphs that repeat it.
- Use occasional expressive headlines when the supporting copy restores clarity.
- A section may create atmosphere, curiosity, or contrast instead of teaching a feature.
- Not every section must mention websites, response, follow-up, and reviews together.

Clarity is required across the page, not inside every individual sentence.

## Useful starting points

Start from a recognisable situation when it helps:

- a strong business represented by a weak website
- a service or treatment page that does not build confidence
- a form that reaches an unattended inbox
- a missed call with no response path
- a quote or consultation request nobody follows up
- good work that never becomes visible proof
- paid attention landing on a vague page

This is a source of insight, not a mandatory opening formula. A homepage may open with a bold promise, an art-directed product demonstration, the work itself, or another strong creative idea.

## Explain the offer plainly

After creating interest, make the difference understandable:

> MindWP designs and builds the website, connects the agreed handling around enquiries, and either hands the setup over or continues managing it.

That line is a meaning test, not required final copy.

Prefer outcomes such as:

- the offer is easier to understand
- proof appears where a decision is being made
- calls, forms, messages, or bookings reach a useful place
- the right person knows an enquiry arrived
- follow-up has an owner and next step
- completed work becomes visible proof

Use technical details when the visitor is actively evaluating delivery, not as the homepage's main source of value.

## Brand language

“Smart website” is the core product phrase. “Connected handling” can explain the response, routing, follow-up, booking, and review layer.

Use both selectively. Repetition can make a useful idea sound like jargon.

Words such as “system,” “automation,” “CRM,” and “AI” are allowed when accurate and helpful. They should not replace the buyer's business outcome or make MindWP look like a tool reseller.

Avoid generic filler such as:

- cutting-edge
- seamless
- robust
- holistic
- growth machine
- all-in-one platform
- comprehensive digital solutions
- skyrocket, dominate, or revolutionise

This is a judgment list, not a mechanical ban on every occurrence.

## Platform and ownership language

The backend platform is not the public hero. It can be named in private sales, proposals, setup, training, and handover conversations when relevant.

Public copy should accurately support both delivery relationships:

- **Client-managed:** the agreed website and connected account are handed over after setup.
- **MindWP-managed:** MindWP continues managing the responsibilities defined in the ongoing agreement.

Do not claim that MindWP always owns the handling system or that the client must always operate it. Do not promise ownership, access, licensing, support, or platform costs beyond what the actual agreement provides.

MindWP does not create or price a client's quotes or invoices. It may support the enquiry status and follow-up around a client-prepared quote.

## Industry language

Home-service and specialist-clinic copy should feel equally respected but genuinely different.

Home-service writing can use projects, surveys, estimates, quoted work, service areas, crews, and homeowner trust. Clinic writing can use treatments, consultations, appointments, patient confidence, practitioner credibility, and careful decision-making.

Focus on considered work rather than emergency or instant-response promises. Do not make medical, treatment-result, compliance, ranking, lead, booking, or revenue guarantees.

## Proof

Evidence should be specific and honest:

- show what is visibly strong in a project
- label demonstration work when it could be mistaken for client work
- anonymise real work when permission is unavailable
- explain process decisions without inventing outcomes
- use measured results only when they are real and supportable

Never fabricate names, businesses, locations, testimonials, ratings, review counts, metrics, rankings, revenue, or before-and-after results.

## Founder privacy

MindWP is brand-led. Public copy may truthfully state that the work is backed by WordPress experience since 2015, but must not reveal a founder name, face, personal location, marketplace profile, account, or biography without explicit approval.

Avoid fictional “small senior team” language unless the team structure is real and the wording has been approved.

## CTA and pricing language

CTAs should feel like useful next steps, not pressure:

- `Request a Website Review`
- `See the work`
- a context-specific invitation to discuss a project

Pricing is private and scope-led. Say that scope and cost are made clear before work starts. Do not publish tier tables or a starting price until the business deliberately approves one.

Avoid “Book a demo,” “Start free trial,” “Get a cheap quote,” or guaranteed-outcome wording.

## Flexible page rhythm

A useful commercial arc is:

```text
Interest → Understanding → Evidence → Confidence → Action
```

This is not a required section order. Pages can use different rhythms, and the homepage especially should alternate visual intensity, explanation, proof, interaction, and breathing room.

Do not repeat the complete offer in every section. Give each section one job and let the full meaning accumulate across the experience.

## Final review

Before approving public copy, check:

- Is the core offer understandable across the page?
- Is the writing specific to the buyer or project?
- Does the copy leave enough room for visual design?
- Are build-and-handover and ongoing management described accurately?
- Are the two industry lanes represented without false specialisation?
- Is personal founder information protected?
- Is all evidence real and honestly labelled?
- Are there unsupported guarantees or inflated competitor claims?
- Could any paragraph be shorter because the design already communicates it?

Protect clarity and credibility, but do not sand away personality.
